<FeaturedWorks>

<Mark color="green">"Art is easy."</Mark> Few phrases are as closely tied to Giuseppe Chiari (Florence, 1926–2007), composer, conceptual artist and the most singular Italian voice within Fluxus. Repeated across statements, exhibitions, scores and  phrasethis exhibition proposes to read it instead as a mantra: a principle to return to, whose meaning deepens with each repetition.

<Mark type="annotate" gesture={15} color="blue">The paradox is evident.</Mark> Like many artists and musicians of the 1960s and 1970s, Chiari had to make difficult art before he could declare art easy. Among his avant-garde contemporaries, many of whom produced rigorous and demanding work, the claim sounded deliberately provocative. Yet it is entirely consistent with an artist shaped by Fluxus and by Gruppo 70, the experimental Florentine collective. In both, the boundaries between art and life, between performer and audience, were open to question.

Behind its apparent simplicity, “Art is Easy” holds a whole: the idea that <Mark type="circle" variant="b">anyone can play,</Mark> that any gesture or sound can become music, and that art lies less in the object than in the experience. This exhibition takes the phrase as the key to Chiari’s complex and idiosyncratic artistic philosophy, and invites visitors to test it out for themselves.

<Mark type="annotate" gesture="coil" color="pink">Chiari never worked within a single discipline. </Mark>A score could become a drawing, a sentence could become a work, and a piano could be something to touch, strike or photograph rather than play. Music, word, image and gesture continually pass into one another in his work, until the boundaries between them disappear. This is what Fluxus artist Dick Higgins called intermedia: art that falls “between media”, in the open space between artistic languages and everyday life. Chiari made that space his own. His works do not ask to be contemplated from a distance. They ask to be experienced, repeated and, often, performed. Seen this way, “art is easy” does not mean art is simple. It means art no longer belongs to specialists and can happen anywhere, to anyone.
