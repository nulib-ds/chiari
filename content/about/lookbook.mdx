# Lookbook

A working reference for how this site looks: the colors, the type, and the marks, followed by placeholder text set the way real pages will be. It is a design tool, not a page for visitors.

## Color

The site reads as printed black-and-white paper that someone has marked up by hand. The page is a pale gray paper and the type is black, with no color fields. Color appears only as paint, and only in a few places.

### Print

Everything that reads as printing is black on the paper, with two steps of Rich Black for quieter text, from Northwestern's primary palette. The paper is a cool off-white with a soft sheen, like light gray film. It is made with gradients only, and they only ever lighten it, so quiet text keeps its contrast everywhere.

### Paint

The paint colors are sampled from the markers on Chiari's own marked-up scores: red, blue, green, yellow and pink. Each is nudged to a midtone so it reads as a mark on the paper or on black. Red, blue, green and pink hold at least 3.8:1 against both, enough for a mark that shows state. Yellow stays light by nature (1.3:1 on paper), so it is decorative only and never the only signal of anything. The paint is for marks, never for text.

The four main sections each own one: Watch red, Play blue, Write green, Collaborate yellow. A section's stroke appears under its menu label on hover and stays under the current section. Pink underlines the current page in the secondary menu and sidebar, and circles the current language; active links themselves stay black. Black paint marks the search field. In copy, `<Mark>` uses the same strokes, sparingly.

<Palette group="paint" />

### Annotations

Hover marks, after the marker and brush gestures Chiari laid over printed scores: scribble blocks, coils, hatching, arcs, underlines, springs, tangles, ticks, broad brush swipes and dabs. There are thirty, three of each. Every search result gets one at random, in a random paint color, laid over the row like marker on a print. Each mark is a mask, so any color can paint any mark. They come from `app/scripts/glyphs/annotations.py`.

## Type

There is one typeface, IBM Plex Sans. Everything else is drawn: Chiari's hand for the occasional heading or quotation, and printed lettering vectorized from his own material.

### IBM Plex Sans

Loaded from Google Fonts. Body, menus, headings and captions are all Plex, in three weights.

### Chiari's hand

Traced from the bold uppercase passages in *Musica senza contrappunto*. J, K, W and Y never appear there, so they are built from his strokes. Set it with `<Hand>`, for a short heading or a short quotation, and never for every one.

Vectorized from the 1974 "Art is Easy" flyer, keeping the chips and pinholes where the ink didn't take. It is used only for the masthead.
